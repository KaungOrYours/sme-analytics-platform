# Experiment 1 — development + final-evaluation full-protocol run

**Status: first full run, 2026-10-20. This is Experiment 1** of the FPR's evaluation chapter — the full
missing-values-only, 3-seed, {clean,15%,35%} protocol frozen in `docs/evaluation_protocol.md`, run once on
both dataset groups. Later experiments (e.g. the conditional second corruption family/sample-size level from
`evaluation_protocol.md` Section 7, or an aggregate-uncertainty pass) get their own numbered section or file
and should say which experiment they are, so the FPR's results chapter and this doc stay in step. Raw per-run
data in `evaluation/results/*.json` (gitignored). This is real output from `evaluation/full_run.py` +
`evaluation/score_rq2.py`, not illustrative numbers — every value below can be regenerated by rerunning those
two scripts. Not yet polished for the FPR; written to capture what's actually there before anything gets
forgotten.

## RQ1 — automated vs. manual baseline (S_test, AUROC)

| Group | N runs | Automated mean | Manual mean | Mean diff (auto−manual) | Automated wins / ties / losses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Development (all 4 datasets) | 36 | 0.9302 | 0.9130 | **+0.0172** | 15 / 3 / 18 |
| Development (excl. `irish`) | 27 | 0.9069 | 0.9309 | **−0.0240** | 6 / 3 / 18 |
| Final-evaluation | 27 | 0.8354 | 0.7659 | +0.0694 | 11 / 15 / 1 |

**Headline finding, flag prominently**: including `irish` makes the automated pipeline look better than the
manual baseline on the development group (+0.0172); excluding it *flips the sign* (−0.0240) and the
loss count (18/27 losses either way, but now a clear majority of a smaller, cleaner set). The entire
development-group RQ1 headline direction depends on whether one leakage-saturated dataset is in the sample.
This is a substantive methodological point for the discussion chapter, not just a footnote: an unweighted,
small-N (4-dataset) pooled average is fragile to exactly the kind of per-dataset artifact described below —
arguably a stronger argument for the dissertation's reframed "study the warnings, not the leaderboard" angle
than a clean win for either pipeline would have been.

Final-evaluation group shows a real, cleaner automated-pipeline edge (automated wins or ties on 26/27 runs)
with no comparable leakage artifact in any of `labor`/`mux6`/`bupa` found so far — worth double-checking the
other two datasets the same way `irish` was checked, before leaning on this as a clean result.

## RQ3 — preliminary transfer (final-evaluation group)

Poor runs (G > 0.05): 3/27 (11.1%).

| Diagnostic | TP | FP | FN | TN | Precision | Recall | False-alarm rate | MCC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n/p < 10 | 3 | 6 | 0 | 18 | 0.333 | 1.0 | 0.25 | **0.50** |
| sigma_cv > 0.10 | 0 | 4 | 3 | 20 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.167 | **−0.147** |

**Headline finding, flag prominently**: this is a clean, coherent preliminary-transfer story and is probably
the strongest single result to lead the RQ2/RQ3 write-up with. The **n/p < 10** diagnostic shows positive
discrimination on both disjoint groups and *improves* on transfer (MCC 0.29 → 0.50, recall 0.667 → 1.0). The
**sigma_cv > 0.10** diagnostic (the one adapted from EvalML, Section 5 of `evaluation_protocol.md`) shows
essentially zero-to-negative discrimination on both groups (MCC −0.148 → −0.147) — worse than a coin flip,
consistently, not a fluke of one sample. Caveat to state every time this is reported: only 3 "poor" runs
exist in each group, so these are small-count confusion matrices and the precision/MCC values have wide
uncertainty — the *consistency* across two disjoint groups is the interesting part, not the point estimates
in isolation. (Aggregate precision/recall/MCC uncertainty across the whole group is explicitly listed as "not
yet specified" in `evaluation_protocol.md` Section 9 — decide a method, e.g. a bootstrap over which runs are
included, before this goes in the FPR as a confidence claim.)

## Case study: `irish`'s `Educational_level` leakage (see `docs/datasets.md` for full writeup)

`Educational_level` perfectly determines `target` in `irish` (zero exceptions, 500/500 rows), discovered
because the automated pipeline hit AUROC=1.0 on every seed at the clean condition — triggering the project's
own "treat 99-100% accuracy as a bug until proven otherwise" rule. Verified as a dataset-level artifact, not a
pipeline bug. Random Forest (the automated pipeline's selected model) exploits the leak fully via threshold
splits; logistic regression (the manual baseline) only partially exploits it because the leak is non-monotonic
in the raw integer coding PMLB uses, capping LR at a believable 0.82–0.90.

**This survives corruption**: `irish`'s automated-pipeline S_test stays at exactly 1.0 even at 35%
missing-value corruption on the training partition (see `evaluation/results/development_rq2_scores.json`) —
the leak is robust enough that imputation doesn't meaningfully disturb it. Worth a sentence on this
robustness specifically, since it's a slightly stronger and more surprising claim than "leakage exists."

Also worth noting directly: at the clean condition, `sigma_cv=0` for all 3 `irish` seeds, so the `sigma_cv >
0.10` diagnostic *cannot* fire there by construction — a saturated run has zero CV variance and is therefore
invisible to a variance-based diagnostic, even though it is arguably the single least trustworthy result in
the whole development group. The app's own excluded `suspected_leakage` warning (ceiling 0.98) would have
caught this; the formally-scored diagnostics in this evaluation would not have, on their own. That contrast
is worth a paragraph in the discussion chapter — it's a real limitation of the chosen diagnostic set, surfaced
by an actual run rather than argued in the abstract.

- **Done 2026-10-20**: sanity-checked `labor`/`mux6`/`bupa` the same way `irish` was checked. `mux6` also
  saturates to S_test=1.0 at the clean condition, but for a different and unconcerning reason: it's the
  classic synthetic 6-bit-multiplexer benchmark, a deterministic nonlinear boolean function with no noise.
  Checked every raw column's single-column purity against `target` — max was 0.625 (`Bit 0`-`Bit 3`), nowhere
  near `irish`'s 1.0 — so there is no single-feature leak; a tree-based model can just learn the deterministic

  function that has to be learned, where it couldn't damage a leak that persists through imputation. `labor`
  and `bupa` show no saturation at all (`bupa` clean S_test ≈ 0.68-0.72, genuinely moderate). The
  final-evaluation group's cleaner RQ1 result does not rest on an unexamined artifact.
